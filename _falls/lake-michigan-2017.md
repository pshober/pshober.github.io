  cameras:
    detail: Two SkySentinel all-sky meteor cameras (Fitchburg WI, Grandville MI) recorded its final flares; Fitchburg also
      tracked the meteor.
    source: Jenniskens et al. 2018 (SkySentinel nodes 38 and 73)
    url: https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.13137
  video:
    detail: Videos from police departments and dash cams, a University of Wisconsin rooftop camera and a Michigan lake-shore
      webcam.
    source: NASA Watch the Skies post with video list
    url: https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/watch-the-skies/2017/02/06/fireball-spotted-northwest-of-chicago-february-6-125-am-cst/
  satellite:
    detail: The GOES-16 weather satellite's lightning mapper detected two flares at the end of the fireball, timed to a
      fraction of a second.
    source: Jenniskens et al. 2018 (GOES-16 GLM)
    url: https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.13137
  infrasound:
    detail: An infrasound station in Manitoba, about 600 miles away, recorded the blast, equal to at least about 10 tons
      of TNT.
    source: NASA Watch the Skies post
    url: https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/watch-the-skies/2017/02/06/fireball-spotted-northwest-of-chicago-february-6-125-am-cst/
  witnesses:
    detail: Hundreds of people saw it; more than 500 reported it to the American Meteor Society, and 74 reported delayed
      sounds.
    source: Jenniskens et al. 2018 (American Meteor Society reports)
    url: https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.13137
